Behind the scenes: the machine learning

PCA map of every offer

Each offer's photo (CLIP, 512 numbers) and title (MPNet, 768 numbers) were compressed with PCA to 32 + 32 numbers. A second PCA squeezes those 64 numbers to 2, so offers can be drawn: close dots look alike to the model.

How it works

Machine learning decides which listings are the same product; price and seller are compared only afterwards.

1Understand the input

A name is searched as is. A store link gives the product name. A photo or screenshot goes to Google Lens, which names the product.

2Collect offers

Up to 40 offers from Indian online stores (Amazon, Flipkart, JioMart, Croma, brand stores and small shops) through Google Shopping, with title, photo, price and rating.

3Read photos and titles

Three neural networks turn each listing into numbers: CLIP (what the photo shows), DINOv2 (which exact object), MPNet (what the title means). PCA compresses them to 32 numbers each.

4Judge every pair

A logistic-regression model trained on 34,250 Shopee listings scores each pair: same item, same item in another colour/pack, or different. A model-word check stops "Pro" or "Gen 2" being mixed in.

5Rank the sellers

Among the exact-same offers: the fair (median) price, the cheapest, and a seller score from match confidence, price fairness, rating, reviews and store reputation.

How accurate is it? Tested on 8 fresh products never used to build the rules (Prestige cooker, Philips trimmer, Mi power bank, Boroplus, Casio calculator, Sony headphones, Wipro bulb, Vaseline lotion): when DupeLens says two listings are the same item, it was the identical item in 23 of 40 pairs (57.5%, 95% CI 40–72.5%) and at least the same product line in 33 of 40 (82.5%); a random pair from the same search was the same item only 3 of 30 times (10%). Labelled blind by Claude, an AI assistant, not humans. Prices, ratings and links come live from Google Shopping (India) at search time. The matching model is DupeLens's own (trained on 34,250 Shopee listings, validation F1 0.864); the seller score is a fixed, transparent rule, not a trained model.
